A regular hour to think about the business side

£120 per session · one hour · video call

Running a practice throws up a steady stream of decisions that have nothing to do with the clinical work. Whether to raise your fees. Whether the directory subscription is worth keeping. What to do about the enquiries that don't convert. Whether now is the right time to add a specialism, or a group, or a second room.

Most practitioners work through those alone, or in the gaps between clients. Growth Sessions are an hour set aside to do it properly, with someone who understands both halves of what you're building.

How it works

You bring what's actually on your mind — a decision you're stuck on, something that isn't working, a plan you want tested before you commit to it. We talk it through, and I tell you what I'd do and why, including the things I'd leave alone.

Afterwards you get a short written summary: what we covered, what we agreed, and the specific things worth working on before we next speak.

How often

Monthly is where most practitioners land. One hour a month is enough to keep momentum without becoming another commitment to manage, and it means each session builds on the last rather than starting cold.

Some people want it weekly — usually during a transition, a relaunch, or a period where decisions are coming quickly. Others want a single session to work through one specific thing and leave it there.

There's no minimum, no block booking and no contract. You book the sessions you want, and you stop when it's no longer useful. £120 per session, whichever way you do it.

What people bring

  • Pricing and whether the current fee matches the work
  • Enquiries that arrive and don't convert
  • Whether to keep paying for a directory, or advertising, or a platform
  • Positioning — who the practice is actually for, and whether the copy says so
  • Adding a specialism, a group, corporate work, or training
  • Admin that's taking more time than it should
  • The commercial side of a decision that's mostly clinical

Why me for this

I'm training as a psychotherapist at CCPE, and I spent thirteen years in commercial marketing and strategy before that, most recently as a Senior Growth Manager at Amazon. Most people come to me for the combination rather than either half.

To be clear about what this is: it's commercial guidance, not clinical supervision, and it isn't a substitute for it.

Sessions or a Blueprint?

Growth Sessions are a thinking partner. They work from what you bring, and they're most useful when you know roughly what the questions are and want someone to help you answer them well.

A Growth Blueprint is a piece of research. I look at your actual website, your search visibility, your directory listings and your competitors, and write up what I find with prioritised recommendations. It's for when you need evidence rather than direction.

They're separate services and neither leads to the other. Plenty of clients only ever do one.

Common questions

Do I have to commit to anything?

No. Book one session or book monthly, and stop whenever you want. There's no block booking, no contract and no subscription.

Is it cheaper if I book monthly?

No — £120 a session either way. Your hour costs the same whichever cadence you choose, so I don't price it differently.

Do I need to have a free call first?

Not unless you want one. You can book a session directly. If you'd rather understand the options before spending anything, a free 15-minute call is there for that.

Is this business supervision?

No, and I'd avoid that framing — supervision is a clinical relationship with its own professional standards, and this isn't one. This is commercial guidance for the business side of a practice.

What do I get in writing?

A short summary after each session: what we discussed, what we agreed, and the specific recommendations to work on before the next one.

Can an organisation use this?

Yes — group practices, CICs and training bodies often find the recurring format works better with more than one person in the room.

What if I run out of things to bring?

Then stop, or move to a longer gap between sessions. I'd rather you paused and came back when something comes up than kept a standing slot out of habit.
