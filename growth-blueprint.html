Your practice, reviewed properly, with a plan you can act on

Scoped per engagement · discovery call, written report, walkthrough

A Growth Blueprint is a full strategic review of how your practice is found, how it's judged, and what's getting in the way of it growing. It's delivered as a written report with recommendations phased into what to do now, next and later, and every finding has evidence behind it.

It starts with a single objective

Before anything gets analysed, we agree what the Blueprint is for. Not “improve my marketing” — one measurable objective over a defined period, in your words, agreed out loud.

That objective then becomes the lens for the whole document. Every recommendation has to earn its place by moving you towards it. Anything that doesn't gets cut, or named explicitly as a later phase. It's the difference between a strategy and a checklist.

What gets looked at

  • Your website — structure, copy, how someone actually makes contact, and whether it holds up on a phone
  • Search visibility — how you appear in Google, and increasingly what AI assistants say when someone asks them to recommend a therapist
  • Directory listings — BACP, UKCP, Counselling Directory, Psychology Today and modality-specific bodies, checked for consistency and for services you offer but haven't listed
  • Google Business Profile and reviews — whether they exist, whether they're claimed, and whether they're visible anywhere that matters
  • Booking and payments — how a client actually books, how many steps it takes, and how you get paid
  • Your competitive set — who a client in your area is genuinely choosing between, what they offer and what they charge

What you receive

A written report, structured around the objective: the context, your competitive set, your current digital footprint, the key findings with evidence for each, and prioritised strategic recommendations phased now / next / later.

I walk you through it on a call. I don't email it over and leave you to interpret it.

A blueprint, not the building

The Blueprint tells you what to build and in what order. It doesn't include building it.

That's deliberate. Plenty of clients take the report and act on it themselves, or brief their own developer from it. Where the work is something I can take on — rebuilding a website, for instance — we agree that separately, afterwards, at no obligation. There's no subscription attached to any of it.

Who it's for

Established practitioners and therapy organisations — solo practices that have plateaued, practices fully booked on word of mouth with no online presence to fall back on, practitioners losing hours to admin, and anyone niching down, adding a modality or launching group or training work.

Who it isn't for

If you're still in training, or newly qualified and not yet seeing clients, a Blueprint won't tell you much you couldn't work out yourself. Come back when there's a practice to look at — the resources are free in the meantime.

If you want a website that looks better without changing anything underneath it, I'm probably not the right person.

Common questions

How much does a Growth Blueprint cost?

It's scoped per engagement, because a solo practice and a training organisation aren't the same piece of work. You get a firm figure in the proposal, after the discovery call, with the scope written down. I don't quote a number before I know what I'm quoting for.

What's the difference between a Growth Session and a Growth Blueprint?

A Growth Session gives you direction based on what you bring, in an hour. A Blueprint gives you evidence — your actual website, search visibility, directory listings and competitors, reviewed in detail and written up. They're separate services and neither leads to the other. If you want a thinking partner, book a session. If you need to know what's actually happening, start with the Blueprint.

Do I have to use you for the implementation?

No, and nothing in the report assumes you will.

Will it tell me whether Counselling Directory or Psychology Today is worth paying for?

Yes, for your practice specifically. The usual answer is that the platform is fine and the profile isn't — a thin bio with generic copy competes badly against a practitioner who has invested in theirs. Worth checking which it is before cancelling anything.

Can you do this for a group practice or training organisation?

Yes. The scope is larger and there are usually more stakeholders, so the discovery stage includes working out who needs to agree the objective.
