Local SEO for therapists: how to rank in your area

21 July 2026 · George Holloway

When someone decides they want to see a therapist, they rarely search for the best therapist in the country. They search for a therapist near them. “Therapist in Hackney.” “Counsellor in Bristol.” “Anxiety therapist near me.”

Local search is where most therapy enquiries actually begin — and it's the area where a relatively small amount of effort makes the biggest difference. Unlike competing nationally, where you're up against thousands of practitioners and established directories, local search is a much smaller playing field. Most therapists in any given area aren't doing this well. Getting it right puts you in front of people who are already looking for exactly what you offer, in exactly the right place.

Here's how to do it.

Set up and complete your Google Business Profile

This is the single most important thing you can do for local visibility, and the most commonly neglected.

When someone searches for a therapist in your area, Google often shows a map with three local results before any website links. Those results come from Google Business Profiles — not from websites. A therapist without a profile won't appear in that map section at all, regardless of how good their website is.

Setting up a profile is free. It takes around thirty minutes to do properly. You'll need to verify your address — Google sends a postcard or offers a video verification option — and once that's done you can complete your profile with your services, hours, location, contact details, and a description of your practice.

The description matters. Write it clearly, include the area you work in and the clients you work with, and avoid the generic language that most profiles default to.

Get your location into your website copy

Search engines rank pages partly based on how relevant they are to a specific search. A website that never mentions where the therapist is based — or buries the location in a contact page footer — is harder to rank locally than one where the location appears naturally throughout the content.

This doesn't mean stuffing your pages with place names. It means writing naturally about where you're based and who you serve. “I work with adults in and around Sheffield” on your homepage does more for local search than a postcode in your footer.

If you work across multiple locations — in person in one place, online more broadly — be specific about both. A page or section dedicated to your in-person location will help that location rank independently.

Get listed on the main directories

Directories like Counselling Directory, Psychology Today, Welldoing, and BACP's therapist finder are where many clients search directly. Being listed on them brings direct traffic from people already looking for a therapist.

But they also contribute to local SEO indirectly. Search engines treat directory listings as signals that your practice exists, is legitimate, and is based where you say it is. Consistent information across multiple directories — the same name, address, and contact details — strengthens that signal.

Inconsistency does the opposite. If your name appears differently across platforms, or your location varies between listings, it creates confusion that works against you.

Ask for reviews where your professional body permits

Google reviews are a ranking factor for local search. A profile with several genuine reviews will typically rank above one with none, all else being equal.

Not all professional bodies permit therapists to solicit reviews, so check your code of ethics before pursuing this. Where it is permitted, a simple message to former clients explaining that a review would be helpful — sent at an appropriate point after ending work — is enough. You don't need many. A handful of honest reviews makes a meaningful difference.

Create location-specific content

If you want to rank for “therapist in [your area],” it helps to have content on your site that's genuinely relevant to that search. This doesn't require a separate page for every nearby town — but a clear, well-written page about your in-person practice, where it's based, and who you see there gives search engines something concrete to work with.

For therapists who cover a wider geographic area — running groups, workshops, or seeing clients across several locations — individual pages for each location can be worth building over time. Each one targets a specific local search and builds your visibility in that area independently.

Keep your information consistent everywhere

This is simple but frequently overlooked. Your name, practice name, address, and phone number should be identical across your website, your Google Business Profile, and every directory listing you have.

Even small variations — “St.” versus “Street,” a different phone number format, an old address that hasn't been updated — create inconsistency that undermines your local search presence. A quick audit of everywhere your practice appears online, to make sure the details match, is worth doing once and then keeping on top of.

Be patient — but start now

Local SEO builds over time. A new Google Business Profile won't put you at the top of local results overnight. What it does is start building the signals that search engines use to rank you — and the earlier you start, the sooner those signals accumulate.

The therapists who appear consistently in local search results aren't necessarily the most qualified or the most experienced. They're the ones who got the basics right early and kept them maintained. That's an achievable standard for any practice, at any stage.
